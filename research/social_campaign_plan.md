# Global University Evergreen Social Media Campaign Plan

**Prepared for:** Global University, Beirut, Lebanon  
**Prepared by:** Manus AI  
**Date:** 24 August 2026  
**Scope:** Organic social system, Meta advertising funnel, website conversion architecture, and measurement requirements.

## Executive recommendation

Global University should move from isolated post-boosting and engagement activity to a single, repeatable admissions engine. The recommended system is an evergreen **“Start Your GU Journey”** funnel that combines student-led proof, clear program guidance, financial-access information, bilingual creative, direct WhatsApp support, and a low-friction website application path. The campaign should run continuously, with creative refreshed weekly and budget moved only after evidence from the correct conversion events.

The current connected Meta portfolio shows the official Global University Facebook and Instagram assets at approximately **38.4K Facebook followers** and **6.1K Instagram followers** at the time of audit. The existing Global University dataset is already present in Events Manager with ID **877982820870158**, but it reports **No integrations**, **No activity was received in the selected time frame**, and **No websites found**. The correct action is therefore to connect the existing asset to the website rather than create a duplicate Pixel. [1] [2]

The account’s last-90-day campaign data shows two active engagement campaigns, both optimized for engagement or messaging rather than website conversion. The Instagram post campaign produced a higher CTR and lower Cost per result than the summer-camp campaign, while the summer-camp campaign delivered more distribution and more messaging conversations because it received more Impressions. This is a useful creative and placement signal, not a reason to make a pause decision from average metrics alone. The next test should preserve the stronger short-form creative pattern while changing the optimization event to a verifiable admissions action.

## Strategic foundation

The core promise should be expressed as **“Quality education within reach, with a clear path from program choice to application.”** This is grounded in the live Global University website’s stated positioning, its three faculties and ten departments, English and Arabic delivery, admissions journey, financial-aid messaging, and direct phone and WhatsApp access. [3] The campaign should not compete with AUB, LAU, USJ, or Balamand by imitating institutional prestige at their scale. It should win by making the decision easier: clear program matching, human guidance, practical academic proof, accessible next steps, and a visibly bilingual Lebanese student experience.

The competitive review shows that the strongest Lebanese university recruitment systems repeatedly combine four elements: authoritative proof, authentic student experience, practical admissions guidance, and a low-friction inquiry channel. AUB makes programs, admissions, financial aid, scholarships, and a tuition calculator highly visible. [4] LAU segments the funnel by applicant type and provides both “Sign Up for Information” and “Schedule a campus visit” options. [5] USJ uses a dedicated orientation office, individual sessions, shadowing, virtual events, a newsletter, a contact form, and WhatsApp. [6] Balamand combines “Apply now,” “Have An Enquiry?,” and live orientation hours. [7] MUBS and MEU demonstrate how a concise value proposition, financial accessibility, leadership or AI-related benefits, and a defined offer can create urgency. [8] [9]

### Positioning framework

## Always-on funnel architecture

The recommended campaign architecture has three layers. The first layer creates qualified demand through short-form video and student-led proof. The second layer answers decision questions for Accounts Center accounts who have already engaged with Global University content or visited the website. The third layer converts high-intent visitors into a genuine admissions inquiry or completed application.

| Layer | Campaign role | Recommended Meta objective | Audiences to test | Creative and CTA | Primary event |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | Introduce GU and generate qualified admissions interest. | Leads or Sales-style website conversion once the event volume is sufficient; begin with Leads if the website lead event is not yet available. | Lebanon-wide broad targeting with separate creative for prospective students and parents/guardians; test age and language assumptions rather than locking them permanently. | 15–30 second Reels, student-led day-in-the-life videos, faculty or lab proof, bilingual captions. CTA: “Find your program” or “Talk to Admissions.” | ViewContent, Contact, then Lead after the website form is real. |
| Consideration | Answer questions that stop an application. | Leads or Traffic for a short validation period only if the conversion event is not yet reliable. | Website visitors, Instagram/Facebook engagers, video viewers, program-page visitors, and application-page visitors. | Requirements explainers, tuition/financial-aid guidance, FAQ carousels, campus walkthroughs, WhatsApp orientation invitation. CTA: “Request information.” | Contact, InitiateCheckout, and Lead. |
| Conversion | Convert high-intent admissions visitors. | Leads optimized for the verified website Lead event. | Application starters, contact-page visitors, admissions-page visitors, and engaged Accounts Center accounts excluding confirmed applicants where that audience is available. | Deadline reminders, document checklist, program match, admissions advisor proof, student outcome/testimonial. CTA: “Start your application” or “Speak with Admissions.” | Lead or CompleteRegistration only after a real backend confirmation exists. |

For the first four weeks, use one consolidated evergreen campaign with a controlled number of ad sets rather than many small campaigns. One ad set should represent student-intent creative and one should represent parent/guardian decision support. If budget is limited, keep the ad set structure broad and let the creative do the segmentation. Use a campaign-level budget when using Meta’s campaign budget automation, and evaluate at the campaign level; do not infer that an individual placement is poor simply because its average metric is higher. Meta’s delivery system optimizes marginal efficiency over time, not a static average. [10]

## Content operating system

The organic calendar should create a steady stream of evidence that can later be reused in paid creative. The recommended weekly rhythm is three short videos, one carousel, one proof or community post, and daily stories built from the same weekly theme. One monthly live orientation or recorded admissions Q&A should become multiple short clips. Content should be produced in English and Arabic according to audience intent: admissions, financial access, and basic guidance should be bilingual; academic and research pieces may lead in English with Arabic subtitles or companion copy; community and cultural content can lead in Arabic with English support where useful.

| Content pillar | Weekly role | Example series | Conversion job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Program clarity | Make it easy to understand what each department leads to. | “Which major fits you?”, “One department in 30 seconds,” “Ask a faculty member.” | Drives ViewContent and program-page visits. |
| Student experience | Replace institutional abstraction with lived proof. | “A day at GU,” student takeovers, campus routes, clubs, lab moments. | Builds retargeting pools and reduces uncertainty. |
| Career and outcomes | Show practical value and the next step after graduation. | Alumni stories, internship or project spotlights, skills in action. | Supports consideration and Lead intent. |
| Access and support | Address affordability, requirements, bilingual guidance, and the process. | Financial-aid FAQ, document checklist, placement-test guidance, advisor answers. | Converts high-intent visitors into Contact or Lead. |
| Trust and institution | Establish credibility without becoming purely ceremonial. | Faculty expertise, recognized programs, partnerships, research, community impact. | Strengthens conversion rate across all stages. |
| Admissions action | Create clear, approved urgency. | Intake dates, open-house invitations, application checklist, deadline countdown. | Drives InitiateCheckout, Lead, or CompleteRegistration when valid. |

### Four-week rolling calendar

| Week | Campaign theme | Priority assets | Paid reuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find your program | Three faculty/department Reels, one program-match carousel, one student introduction, daily FAQ stories. | Prospecting video and retargeting program carousel. |

## Creative briefs for the first production sprint

### “Which GU program fits you?”

Open with a real student question such as “Do you prefer technology, health, business, or education?” Show three fast program pathways, a current student or faculty member, and a final screen that says **“Tell us your goal. GU Admissions will help you choose the right path.”** The destination should be a mobile-first program guidance page with a short form or WhatsApp option, not a generic homepage.

### “A day at GU”

Use a student-led vertical video that shows arrival, a class or lab, peer interaction, a campus detail, and one sentence about support from faculty. Use subtitles in the second language. The final frame should display the verified website domain and one CTA only: **“Explore programs”** or **“Talk to Admissions.”**

### “Application in five steps”

Turn the existing site journey into a five-card sequence: choose a program, check requirements, placement test, submit the application, and explore financial aid. [3] The first four cards should answer the question; the fifth should route to the correct application page and offer WhatsApp support for uncertainty.

### “Student or parent FAQ”

Create separate cuts of the same FAQ for student-intent and parent/guardian-intent audiences. Questions should be approved by Admissions and should cover program fit, documents, language, fees or aid, and who to contact. Use a direct answer in the first two seconds and one CTA at the end.

## Measurement and decision rules

The website should be treated as the source of truth for admissions actions, while Meta is used for delivery and conversion optimization. The current application page simulates a successful submission in client-side code and does not yet send the data to a backend. Therefore, a **Lead** or **CompleteRegistration** event must not be fired on the simulated success screen. The code implementation in this repository records safe page and intent events and deliberately leaves true Lead confirmation for the point at which the admissions form returns a real backend success response.

| Funnel signal | Event | Definition | Optimization use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page visit | PageView | A page loads or a client-side route changes. | Diagnostics and retargeting. |
| High-value content | ViewContent | Admissions, program, or application content is viewed. | Consideration audience and early signal. |
| Application intent | InitiateCheckout | A valid admissions application is submitted for processing, or an application-start CTA is selected. | Mid-funnel optimization while Lead volume is being established. |
| Direct inquiry | Contact | A visitor selects WhatsApp, telephone, or email contact. | Practical conversion signal for admissions. |
| Qualified inquiry | Lead | Admissions receives a real inquiry or application record through a functioning backend or validated lead form. | Primary conversion event. |
| Completed application | CompleteRegistration | The admissions system confirms a completed application. | Use only after server or backend confirmation. |

The first four weeks should be used to establish event integrity. Monitor Events Manager diagnostics and the website’s own admissions records together. Do not judge the campaign using only CTR or Link clicks. The primary scorecard should be based on verified Contacts, Leads, application starts, completed applications, Cost per result, and downstream enrollment quality. If conversion volume is too low for stable optimization, keep the campaign live, consolidate data, and test creative and offer hypotheses rather than making rapid budget changes.

## Budget scenarios

The following are planning scenarios, not a recommendation to launch without approval. The current account historically used daily budgets around $5 and $10 on individual campaigns, so the starter plan should consolidate rather than multiply that spend across many ad sets. [2]

| Scenario | Daily budget | Monthly planning range | Recommended use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Validation | $10 | Approximately $300 | Establish event quality, test two creative angles, and learn which inquiry path produces real admissions responses. |
| Growth | $20 | Approximately $600 | Run prospecting and retargeting with enough room for creative rotation and a controlled parent/guardian test. |
| Intake push | $30–$50 | Approximately $900–$1,500 | Use only around a confirmed intake deadline, open day, approved scholarship, or verified admissions capacity. |

Because the account timezone is reported as America/Los_Angeles while Global University operates in Lebanon, campaign scheduling and daily reporting cutoffs must be checked against Lebanon time before launch. The ad account’s current recommendations endpoint returned no recommendations, so there is no account-level Meta recommendation to override in this plan. [2]

## Operating workflow and governance

The campaign should be managed through a weekly production and optimization cycle. Admissions should approve factual claims, dates, tuition and financial-aid language, accreditation wording, scholarship terms, and student or alumni permissions. The content team should publish the organic evidence first, then promote only assets that match a defined hypothesis. The performance owner should review the correct event level, avoid mixing objectives when aggregating Cost per result, and record changes in a simple test log.

The minimum weekly dashboard should include Amount spent, Impressions, Reach, Frequency, Clicks (all), Link clicks, CTR, Link click-through rate, CPM, Cost per result, Contacts, Leads, application starts, completed applications, and enrollment-quality notes. Where the API returns unavailable values, report “Data not available” or “N/A”; do not estimate. For CBO-style campaigns, evaluate at campaign level; for ad-set budget campaigns with automatic placements, evaluate at ad-set level. [10]

## Launch gates

The campaign should not be launched as a website-conversion campaign until the following gates are met: the existing Global University dataset is connected to the live website; PageView appears in Events Manager; ViewContent fires on admissions and program pages; Contact fires on WhatsApp, phone, and email links; the application path fires InitiateCheckout only after valid application intent; the website team confirms that the application form writes to a real backend; and Lead or CompleteRegistration is mapped to that backend confirmation. Meta’s official installation guidance also recommends considering Conversions API alongside the Pixel to improve measurement and performance, but server-side implementation should follow only after the actual admissions data flow and privacy process are confirmed. [11]

## Assumptions requiring Global University confirmation

The current plan assumes the priority is undergraduate recruitment for the next available intake, with graduate and continuing-education campaigns added as separate creative tracks. It also assumes that Admissions can respond to WhatsApp or web inquiries during defined service hours, that student and alumni permissions can be obtained, and that official scholarship and deadline claims will be supplied in writing. The monthly budget, intake deadline, priority faculties, target geographic markets outside Lebanon, preferred lead destination, and required Arabic/English ratio remain to be confirmed before media buying.
